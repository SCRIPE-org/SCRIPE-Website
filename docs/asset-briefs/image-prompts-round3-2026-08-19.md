## Where this image goes and why it is a room, not a screenshot

`/platform` makes one claim that visibly begs for a picture and does not have one:

> **"Every module resolves into one dashboard."**
> *Revenue, members, reservations, attendance, events, payments and insights — read together, reconciled the same day.*

Right now that headline is followed by three numbers and nothing else.

The obvious-looking answer — a picture of the dashboard — is the wrong one, and deliberately
so. A generated screenshot of a product UI is **fabricated product evidence**: it shows
software that does not look like that, to a buyer who will reasonably believe it does. This
programme already ruled on the milder version of this once (the Football Intelligence
visual had to carry an explicit `ILLUSTRATIVE` label), and a photorealistic fake console
is well past where a label rescues it.

So the photograph is of **the people the dashboard is for** — the operations side of a
sports campus, mid-shift. That is honest, it is the one thing the whole page is missing
(there is not a single human being on it), and it says "same-day close" far better than a
mocked-up chart would.

**Framing constraint:** any screen visible in frame must be **out of focus, off-axis, or
clipped** — legible enough to read as "someone is working in a system", never legible
enough to read as a claim about ours.

## 1. ⭐ NEEDED — Platform page: the operations desk

> Interior photograph of the operations counter at a large modern sports campus, late afternoon. Two staff members behind a long pale-stone reception desk, seen from the side and slightly behind at shoulder height, so no face is toward the camera — one leaning over a desktop monitor, one turned toward the glass wall. Beyond the glass: a floodlit multi-sport campus at dusk, courts and turf visible but soft, well out of focus. The monitors are angled away from the lens and thrown out of focus — their content is unreadable, reading only as cool screen glow on the staff's faces and the desk surface. Warm interior light against the cool blue dusk outside, the two temperatures meeting on the desk. Shallow depth of field (35mm, f/2), the sharp plane on the near staff member's hands and the desk edge. Calm, competent, unstaged — a normal shift, not a press photo. 21:9 crop, horizontal.

**If your generator will give you a second framing of the same scene, this helps mobile:**

> The same operations counter and the same dusk campus beyond the glass, recomposed vertically: the desk running from the bottom-left corner up into the frame, one staff member at the monitor in the lower third, the floodlit campus filling the upper half through the glass. 4:5 crop, vertical.

*(If the vertical is inconvenient, skip it — a center crop of the 21:9 is workable, just less composed.)*

## 2. Optional alternate — if the desk scene comes out stiff or stocky

Interiors with people are the hardest thing to generate without it looking like stock
photography. If prompt 1 keeps producing something that feels like a brochure, this is the
same idea with the people removed and the story told by the room instead:

> Interior photograph of an empty operations room at a sports campus just after close, late blue hour. A long desk with three dark monitors, a wheeled chair pushed slightly back, a lanyard and a steel water bottle left on the surface — the room a minute after the last person left. Floor-to-ceiling glass along one wall showing the floodlit campus outside, courts and pitches still lit, soft and out of focus. The only light in the room is what comes through the glass plus one warm overhead pool of light on the desk. Cool blue exterior against that single warm interior note. 35mm, f/2.8, sharp on the desk objects. Quiet, end-of-day, human-but-unpeopled. 21:9 crop, horizontal.

---

### Note on both themes

Whichever we use gets **one** asset, not two. The site's rule since the dark/light decision
is that framed photography must read correctly in both themes and the *frame* adapts, not
the picture. A dusk interior with a warm-vs-cool split works in both — which is exactly why
both prompts above are lit that way rather than being straightforwardly bright or dark.
